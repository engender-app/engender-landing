# engender landing page: a reframing proposal

Written 2026-09-29 against `content/en/landing.md`, `.agents/product-marketing.md` v12, and the Journal repository's `PRODUCT.md`, `SCREENS.md`, `CONTEXT.md` and `docs/ui-copy.md`. Everything inside a blockquote is proposed copy. Everything outside one is commentary.

The design stays. Every section below maps onto a section the page already has; the only new element is a four-cell promise strip under the hero actions.

## Why the current page reads as uninteresting

The page opens with a dictionary entry and one abstract line, "A transition tracker with a journal at its heart". A visitor who has never heard of the app learns what the word means before they learn what the app would do for them. The pun is good and should stay, but right now it is the page's first move rather than its ornament.

The four things you want understood at a glance are all on the page. None of them is above the fold. "Free, and stays that way" is the third paragraph of the overview and again in Support, at the very bottom. "No account, no server" is one clause in the subheadline and then a blue block halfway down. Surgeries get two words ("surgery recovery"), eras are not on the page at all, tryouts are one word in a list, the clinician summary is "print a summary for a clinician" inside the Health paragraph. "One trans person wrote this" is one sentence, three paragraphs in.

The feature section is organised by the app's information architecture (Writing it down, Reading it back, Around the journal, Keeping it) rather than by what the reader came looking for. A person who searched "HRT tracker" has to read the Health paragraph to discover that doses, labs and a hormone curve are in there. The section headings ("What engender is", "What it does", "Keeping it") tell a scanner nothing.

The register is written for the reader who audits: mechanism first, conclusion second, limit in the same breath. That is right for the privacy page and it is what earns trust from the person who will check. A non-technical reader needs the order reversed on the landing page: the conclusion first ("I never have your journal, so I cannot sell it"), the mechanism as the proof. The marketing doc bans bare adjectives like "safe" and "private". It does not ban conclusions, as long as the mechanism sits next to them.

So the fix is front-loading and reordering, not a louder voice. The voice rules in v12 (one person, first person singular, no urgency, no invented proof, no talking about the copy) all hold below.

## The new skeleton

Hero field: nameplate (unchanged), headline, subheadline. After the field: Start journal, the channel badges, then the promise strip (new). Overview, retitled. The feature section becomes a grid of the eight things you named, in your order, followed by the existing screenshot tour and one paragraph covering everything else. The blue privacy field, rewritten conclusion-first. The tail groups (Keeping it, On your phone, If you need to be careful, How it looks) stay as they are, with one factual fix. Acquisition stays. Support is retitled and leads with who made it.

## Hero

The nameplate block stays exactly as it is. The headline underneath is where the work happens.

> Your whole transition, in one place, on your own device.

Two alternatives, if that one reads too flat:

> Keep your transition in one place that only you can open.

> One app for the whole of your transition. It lives on your device, and it is free for good.

Subheadline:

> Journal, stats, HRT, surgeries, tryouts, eras, voice practice and a summary to hand your clinician. No account, no server, no price. One trans person wrote it for other trans people.

That is the four messages in three sentences, visible before anyone scrolls. "HRT" is not the app's own word (the app says medication, regimen, dose), but v12 already says to meet people in the language they searched with, and "HRT tracker" is that language.

## The promise strip (new)

Four cells directly under the Start journal button and the badges, so the reader has the whole argument before the first section heading. Each cell is a short heading and one to three sentences. On a phone they stack.

> **Free, for good.**
> No price, no plan, no ads. The licence is GPLv3, which cannot be revoked, so nobody can charge for this later, and that includes me.

> **Nothing to sell.**
> There is no account and no server. Your journal is stored on your device, encrypted, and I never see it. I cannot sell what I do not have.

> **All of it, in one place.**
> Journal, stats, HRT, surgeries, tryouts, eras, voice practice and a printable summary for your clinician.

> **By a trans person, for trans people.**
> One person made this. There is no company behind it.

"I cannot sell what I do not have" is the one closing turn I would keep on the page. It is the exact claim you asked for ("unable to use or sell"), stated as the mechanism rather than as a promise, which is what v12's objections table already does.

## Overview

Retitle "What engender is" to "What this is". The current lede repeats the hero; this one answers "is this for me".

> engender is where you keep your transition: how you feel from day to day, what you take, what you are trying, what is coming up and what has already happened. It is a journal first. Around the journal sit the records that otherwise end up spread across a notes app, a spreadsheet, a photo folder and a letter from a clinic.

> Nothing in it decides anything about you. You name your own scales and pick what to track, and the app draws what you put in without grading it. It never suggests a dose, never colours a number red and never tells you which way you are meant to be going.

> One trans person wrote this for other trans people. It is free software: there is no price, no plan and nothing to upsell, and the licence keeps it that way.

## What it holds

Replaces "What it does" and its three subsections. A grid of eight cards, in the order you gave. Each card is a heading and two to four sentences. The screenshot tour, with its "invented data" note, follows the grid unchanged.

> **Journal**
> How today felt. A mood, a note, your own scales, tags, photos, a voice memo, and where in your body dysphoria or euphoria sat. One of those is enough. A day where all you managed was a mood is still a day you logged.

> **Look back**
> Whether anything is changing is hard to see day to day and easy to see over months. A heat map of your year, a chart for each scale, how days with a tag compare to days without, a wrapped for any week, month or year, and what you wrote a year ago today.

> **HRT**
> Your medication schedule with the next dose and how much is left, doses logged in one tap, lab results drawn as a chart, and a modelled hormone curve. The app never suggests a dose or shows a reference range.

> **Surgeries**
> A journal per procedure: consults, the date itself, recovery, dilation where it applies, and photos that stay inside the app rather than in your camera roll.

> **Tryouts**
> Try a name, a pronoun set, a style or a garment. Give it a start date, log how it feels as you go, and close it when you know.

> **Eras**
> Name the chapters of your own timeline, whatever they were for you. They draw as bands behind your charts and milestones, so a reading sits against the chapter you were in when you wrote it.

> **Voice**
> Practise, record the same passage each time, and compare with your own earlier takes. Pitch, resonance and speaking rate describe the recording without grading your voice.

> **Clinician summary**
> Bring your doses, labs, measurements and changes to an appointment as one printed summary, over a range you choose, with only the sections you choose.

Then one paragraph for everything the grid leaves out, so the breadth is stated without another wall of bold leads:

> Also in there: milestones with countdowns ahead and anniversaries behind, letters that open on a date you choose, a roadmap of the legal and medical steps in Poland with room for your own, binder and tucking wear time, hair removal sessions, measurements and sizes, appointment prep, Safe Space with your good days and your own comfort list, a directory of trans organisations and helplines you can read offline, one photo library across all of it, and a place for referrals and court orders. If you already have a year in Daylio, it imports.

## The privacy field

Same blue block, same link to the privacy page. Conclusion first, mechanism second, limit in the same breath.

> Your journal stays on your device. There is no engender account, no server, and no copy of your entries anywhere but where you put them. I cannot read your journal, sell it or hand it over, because I never have it.

> On your device it is encrypted, and you choose what opens it: a passphrase, a PIN, your fingerprint or face, or the device's own lock. On Android the app does not even ask for internet permission. In a browser, the web host sees that the app was loaded and nothing of what you write.

> What that protects, and what it does not (an unlocked phone in someone else's hands, for one), is on its own page.

> [What engender protects, and what it does not]

The Android line is true: `AndroidManifest.xml` declares no `INTERNET` permission and the comment there says the absence is the point. It is the single most persuasive privacy fact on the page for a non-technical reader, because they can check it themselves in the Play listing's permission list. The web line keeps the never-list's separation between app-shell traffic and journal data.

"If you need to be careful" stays as written. Its current copy already follows v12's rule that the controls are presented as available rather than expected.

## Support

Retitle to "Who made this". Lead with the person, then the licence, then the two existing paragraphs.

> One trans person made this, and that person is me. There is no company, so there is no support desk and nobody to sell it to.

> You can read it. engender is free software under the GPLv3. Do not take my word for anything on this page: the source is public. Go and look, or get someone you trust to look.

> The licence means you can run it, change it and pass it on, and it cannot be taken back. If I stop, someone else is free to pick the code up, and your Archives are a documented format rather than something only this app can open.

> If something is broken, tell me what happened and what you expected instead. That is usually enough to find it.

> Do not send me your journal. Not an Archive, not a screenshot with your entries in it, not a log from a session where you were writing. I will never ask for any of it. If something asks, it is not me.

## Where this departs from product-marketing.md v12

You own the doc, so these are decisions rather than problems, but each one should be written into v13 rather than left for the next copy ticket to trip over.

The frame "one trans person wrote this for other trans people" currently appears once, in the overview, and v12 says it is not a refrain. This proposal puts it in the hero subheadline, the promise strip, the overview and Support: four times. I would cut it to three by dropping it from the overview's third paragraph, since the strip sits directly above. Three is still a change to the rule.

The positioning line "A transition tracker with a journal at its heart" was decided on 2026-09-25 (ticket 09), with custom scales, Voice benchmarks and care records as the breadth on the first screen. This proposal replaces the headline and swaps that breadth list for your eight. Both are v12 decisions that need overriding.

"100% free" and "will never be able to" are in your brief. Neither phrase is on the page above. v12 bans "100 percent private" as security theatre and the same reasoning applies to a percentage on "free": "Free, for good", plus the reason the licence makes it so, is a stronger sentence than a number. "Unable" is written as "I cannot sell what I do not have" and "I never have it", which is the same claim with its mechanism attached.

"Voice training" is not the app's word. The screen is practice plus benchmarks, and ADR-0059 tightly restricts what the pitch figure may say. The card says "Voice" and "practise".

## Facts to fix or verify before shipping

The site says eight palettes. `src/lib/theme/palettes.css` in the Journal repository defines sixteen, and `SCREENS.md` says all sixteen stay. Either the site is out of date or the extra eight are not in the shipped build; either way, "eight" on the landing page currently contradicts the spec the copy is supposed to be written from.

The cards above name a modelled hormone curve, a surgery journal with dilation, eras drawn behind charts and milestones, tryouts with a felt-sense log, and a clinician summary with a range and section picker. All five are in `SCREENS.md` and `CONTEXT.md` as shipped. The sixteen-palette line is the only place I found the site and the spec disagreeing.

Ko-fi. Settings has a "Support on Ko-fi" row. The page says "no price" and that is true, but if a reader finds a donate link after reading "nothing to upsell" it can feel like a catch. One sentence in Support ("If you want to buy me a coffee there is a link in Settings, and that is the whole of the business model") would close that gap. Your call whether it belongs on the page at all.
